What to Say When Schools Try to Shut You Down

These aren't hypothetical. These are things that actually get said in IEP meetings — designed to make you back down, feel outnumbered, or accept less. None of the responses below are confrontational. Every one is a request for evidence or documentation.

When they cite "policy"
"I'm sorry, we can't do that. It's against our policy."
"Can you share a copy of that policy with me? And can we note in the IEP that I made this request and it was denied due to district policy?"
When they say it's already being done
"We don't need to put that in the IEP. We already do that for him."
"If it's important enough to do, it's important enough to document. What if my child changes schools? What if there's a new teacher? I'd like it in the IEP so it follows him."
When they rush you to sign
"We just need your signature so we can start services."
"I'd like to take it home and review it. I want to make sure I understand everything before I consent."

These are 3 of the 9 scripts in the full IEP Fight Kit. The full version includes responses for when they want to vote on your child's IEP, when they dismiss your outside evaluation, when they say "we don't do that here," and more.

The One Sentence That Changes Everything

Under federal law (IDEA §300.503), whenever a school proposes or refuses to change your child's services, placement, or evaluation, they must explain why — in writing. This is called Prior Written Notice. Schools almost never offer it voluntarily. You have to ask.

Here's the sentence:

"I'd like to receive Prior Written Notice of that decision before we wrap up today."

Say it every time they deny a request. You don't need to explain why. You don't need to argue. Just ask for the paper trail. One of two things will happen: they put their reason in writing (giving you evidence if you need to escalate), or they change their mind — because a weak reason looks worse on paper.
